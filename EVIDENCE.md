# EVIDENCE: the peak and diversity case

The numbers behind the claim in [ABOUT.md](ABOUT.md) that the binding constraint on electrifying
heat and transport in the UK is **peak and diversity, not annual energy**. Every figure here is
taken from the study named below, and every one of them is checkable. Where a figure carries its own
source, that source is named beside it.

Full study: The real peak challenge of electrifying heat and transport in the UK.
https://www.ventusltd.com/post/the-real-peak-challenge-of-electrifying-heat-and-transport-in-the-uk

## Energy, with its year attached

| quantity | value | note |
|---|---|---|
| UK annual energy use, 2019 | 141 Mtoe | Office for National Statistics |
| the same figure in electrical units | 1,644 TWh | at the IEA conversion of 11.63 TWh per Mtoe |
| UK demand after electrification | 500 to 700 TWh a year | projection |
| additional demand from AI and data centres by 2040 | 50 to 100 TWh a year | projection |

Later years differ from 2019, and the 2019 figure must always be read with its year attached.

## Peak, which is a different quantity

Annual energy is measured in TWh. Peak is measured in GW. They are not interchangeable, and the
whole argument turns on the difference.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| present UK winter peak demand | 48.3 GW |
| present derated generation capacity | 74.8 GW |
| naive peak, 28 million homes at 3.5 kW of heat pump | 98 GW |
| naive peak, 20 million vehicles at 7.4 kW of charging | 148 GW |
| naive combined stress test | 246 GW |
| heat pump winter peak once diversity is applied | about 40 GW |
| maximum demand per home once diversity is applied | about 1.7 kW, per Energy Systems Catapult |
| manageable peak ceiling with planning | 90 to 100 GW |

The naive number and the realistic number differ by roughly a factor of six, and the whole of that
difference is **diversity**: the fact that not every appliance runs at once. Diversity is a
calculation, not an opinion, and it is the calculation the grid is planned on.

## Why electrification reduces energy even as it raises peak

| technology | figure |
|---|---|
| heat pump, seasonal coefficient of performance | 3.5 to 5.0 |
| heat pump against a gas boiler | around 70 per cent less energy |
| 3.5 kW electrical input at SCOP 3.5 | 12.25 kW of heat |
| electric drivetrain, energy converted to motion | about 70 per cent |
| internal combustion engine, the same measure | about 25 per cent |

This is the reason the two quantities move in opposite directions. Total energy falls, because a
heat pump and an electric motor waste far less of what they are given. Peak electrical demand rises,
because the energy that used to arrive as gas or as liquid fuel now arrives as electricity, through
a grid built for a smaller peak.

## A note on the charts in the study

The study's graphs carry part of the argument, and at present they cannot be read by a machine, a
screen reader, or a search engine. Any figure that exists only inside an image is, by this
repository's first law, a candidate rather than a fact. The remedy is the same as everywhere else
here: a caption stating what the chart shows and in what units, and the underlying numbers published
beside it.
